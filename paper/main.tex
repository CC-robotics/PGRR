\title{PGRR: Planning-Guided Failure-Triggered Recovery and Rejoin\\
for Dynamic Social Navigation}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}

\begin{abstract}
Classical navigation is reliable in routine mapped environments yet remains
brittle under reciprocal pedestrian interactions.  We introduce PGRR, a
failure-triggered recovery layer that preserves Nav2 DWB for nominal navigation
and invokes a learned policy only when observable risk or progress rules persist.
Rather than commanding velocity, the policy selects among 21 temporary subgoals
and four interpretable recovery modes.  A planning mask rejects infeasible
decisions, and an independent stopping-distance supervisor may override motion.
A privileged
short-horizon planner supplies recovery demonstrations for behavior cloning and
DAgger; deployment uses only LiDAR and navigation history.  Our locked Gazebo
evaluation comprises \PGRRTotalEpisodeCount{} episodes across eight interaction
families and three densities.  On \PGRRMainPairCount{} matched Base--PGRR
conditions, PGRR records no collision termination, compared with
\PGRRBaseCollisionRate{} for Base, a paired difference of
\PGRRCollisionDifference{} (95\% bootstrap interval
[\PGRRCollisionCILow{}, \PGRRCollisionCIHigh{}], Holm-adjusted
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}}\PGRRCollisionHolmP$).  Goal success is \PGRRSuccessRate{} versus
\PGRRBaseSuccessRate{} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=\PGRRSuccessHolmP$).  On held-out
recovery states, DAgger raises top-1 expert agreement from 88.5\% to 92.2\% and
reduces expert-cost regret from 0.271 to 0.031.  These results demonstrate an
auditable route to contact-averse, interpretable recovery while identifying
persistent reciprocal blockages as the principal remaining challenge.
\end{abstract}
