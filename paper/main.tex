\begin{abstract}
Classical navigation is dependable in routine mapped environments but can
freeze, oscillate, or collide in reciprocal pedestrian interactions.  We present
PGRR, a bounded recovery layer that leaves Nav2 DWB in charge of nominal motion
and invokes learning only after an observable failure trigger.  A compact policy
selects among 21 temporary subgoals and four interpretable modes; a
planning mask rejects infeasible actions and an independent supervisor may stop
unsafe motion.  Training uses a privileged short-horizon rollout expert,
behavior cloning, and DAgger, whereas deployment uses only LiDAR and navigation
history.  A locked Gazebo study contains \PGRRTotalEpisodeCount{} logical
episodes across eight interaction families and three densities.  In the
\PGRRMainPairCount{} paired Base--PGRR tests, Base and PGRR achieve success rates
of \PGRRBaseSuccessRate{} and \PGRRSuccessRate{}, and collision rates of
\PGRRBaseCollisionRate{} and \PGRRCollisionRate{}, respectively.  The paired
success-rate difference is \PGRRSuccessDifference{} (95\% bootstrap interval
[\PGRRSuccessCILow{}, \PGRRSuccessCIHigh{}], Holm-adjusted
$p=\PGRRSuccessHolmP$).  The results quantify a condition-dependent
safety--completion trade-off rather than universal superiority.  All raw
streams, exclusions, statistics, figures, and manuscript numbers are generated
under a frozen manifest.
\end{abstract}
