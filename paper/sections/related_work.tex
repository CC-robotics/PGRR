\subsection{Classical and Social Navigation}
The dynamic-window approach searches dynamically feasible velocity commands over
a short horizon~\cite{fox1997dwa}; Nav2 provides a modern ROS2 navigation
system in which DWB implements this family of local control~\cite{macenski2020marathon2}.
Reciprocal methods such as ORCA~\cite{vandenberg2011orca} and learned crowd-aware
policies such as SARL~\cite{chen2019sarl} instead model interaction more directly.
The broader social-navigation literature emphasizes that collision avoidance,
comfort, legibility, and task efficiency are distinct objectives
~\cite{mavrogiannis2023survey}.  PGRR retains DWB as the nominal
controller and evaluates geometric clearance and discomfort time; it does not
claim to model all social norms.

\subsection{Hybrid Planning and Recovery}
All-in-One learns a continuous switch among complete navigation planners
~\cite{kastner2021allinone}, whereas DR-MPC learns a residual around model-predictive
control~\cite{han2024drmpc}.  Both demonstrate the value of hybrid model-based and
learned navigation.  PGRR differs in authority and output: intervention is
failure-triggered, and the learned output is a temporary subgoal or an
interpretable recovery mode rather than a nominal planner choice or velocity
residual.

Navigation recovery itself is not new.  Del Duchetto \emph{et al.} learn local
failure detection and recovery from human demonstrations
~\cite{delduchetto2018recovery}; Mohammad \emph{et al.} predict planning failure
and search for a recovery state using a Gaussian-process model
~\cite{mohammad2024gprecovery}.  Our focus is automatic privileged-planner labels,
planning-mask enforcement, and aggregation on states
induced by the recovery learner.  DAgger supplies the standard reduction for this
sequential distribution shift~\cite{ross2011dagger}.  Invalid-action masking has
also been analyzed in policy-gradient learning~\cite{huang2022invalidmasking};
here it is used as a deterministic planning constraint for imitation inference,
not as a safety proof.

\subsection{Simulation and Evaluation}
Arena-Bench introduced reproducible dynamic-obstacle evaluation
~\cite{kastner2022arenabench}, and later Arena releases expanded social-navigation
scenarios and ROS2 support~\cite{kastner2024arena3,shcherbyna2024arena4,shcherbyna2025arena5}.
Our installed, pinned Humble profile is an earlier Gazebo fallback rather than
Arena~5.0.  We therefore use the later releases only as platform context and
fully disclose the deterministic pedestrian proxy used by the experiments.
