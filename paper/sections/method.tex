\subsection{Problem and Observable State}
Let a differential-drive robot navigate a known static occupancy map from pose
$x_0$ to goal $g$ while a classical planner $B$ supplies command $u^B_t$.  PGRR
must attempt recovery without using pedestrian truth at deployment.  Its history
$o_t$ contains five 180-beam LiDAR scans, goal distance and bearing, eight path
waypoints in the robot frame, robot velocity, $u^B_t$, ten-step goal-distance and
angular-velocity histories, a one-hot planner status, and four rule scores.
Ground-truth pedestrian state is isolated in a privileged record used only for
expert labeling and evaluation.  Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} separates these
training-only signals from the observable deployment loop.

\subsection{Failure Trigger and Recovery State Machine}
The detector computes collision-risk, freeze, oscillation, and deadlock scores.
Collision risk combines braking distance, 1.5-s time to collision, absolute
near-field clearance, and a 0.5-s radial closing trend.  The absolute trigger
uses the narrow forward sector; an off-axis return must retain a consistent
bearing and close faster than the range change explained by ego motion.  This
keeps a static corner shelf from impersonating a moving obstacle while the
independent footprint guard remains active.  Freeze requires more
than 1 m remaining, less than 0.15 m displacement over 3 s, and a planner request
for motion.  Oscillation requires at least six angular-velocity sign changes
after a 0.08 rad/s deadband and less than 0.20 m progress over 4 s.  Deadlock uses
less than 0.15 m displacement and less than 0.15 m goal progress over 4 s, an
obstacle within 1.2 m, and low robot speed.  Motion rules are disabled for the
first 4 s after the synchronized
episode handshake; collision evidence remains active.

Figure~\ref{fig:state-machine} shows the complete bounded state machine.
NORMAL, PENDING, RECOVERY, and REJOIN are nonterminal; EMERGENCY-STOP has
highest priority and resumes through RECOVERY, REJOIN, or NORMAL as indicated
by the saved state and current score.  Only FAILED and SUCCEEDED are terminal.
The final configuration confirms entry above $\tau_{on}=0.65$ in one decision
frame and requires four frames below $\tau_{off}=0.35$ plus valid goal progress
to leave recovery.  Decisions occur at 2 Hz and velocity control at 10 Hz, with
a 0.5-s minimum hold and 2-s cooldown.  A normal learned option is bounded by
8 s, a still-active directional-yield option by 30 s, and the entire unresolved
recovery--rejoin sequence by 45 s.  Each REJOIN is bounded by 5 s, permits at
most two returns to RECOVERY within the sequence, and the state machine permits
at most four consecutive recovery activations.  The original goal is stored
before intervention and reissued whenever a bounded temporary option hands
control to REJOIN; this transition does not require Nav2 to have reached the
temporary target.  A recovery is counted successful only when REJOIN then
returns to NORMAL with the original goal active, the score below $\tau_{off}$,
and valid progress; no extra post-transition protection window is assumed.

\subsection{Recovery Actions, Mask, and Safety Supervisor}
The 25 actions are 21 robot-relative subgoals
\begin{equation}
 (r,\theta)\in\{0.6,1.0,1.4\}\,\mathrm{m}
 \times\{-90,-60,-30,0,30,60,90\}^{\circ},
\end{equation}
and WAIT, BACKUP, REPLAN, and CONTINUE.  For action $a$, a Boolean mask
$M(o_t,a)$ rejects occupied or off-map endpoints, disconnected grid components,
an obstructed swept LiDAR segment, insufficient rear observability, excessive
global-path deviation, or unavailable replanning.  WAIT is the final safe
fallback, so at least one action remains legal.  Before softmax, masked logits
are set to the minimum representable value.

During an active learned directional-yield latch, LiDAR bearings and candidate
endpoints are represented in the local task-path frame.  Comparing the oldest
and newest scans of the five-frame stack, a side is marked as closing when at
least six valid returns in its $5^{\circ}$--$60^{\circ}$ sector decrease by at
least 0.20 m and their current ranges are at most 4.0 m.  Evidence updates are
suspended when $|\omega|>0.20$ rad/s.  The yield latch itself releases only
after three distinct scans show at least 0.90 m clearance inside a
$\pm15^{\circ}$ corridor centered on the observable local path tangent while
the collision score is clear.  Retained side evidence removes only
already-legal endpoints directed toward the observed closing side.  After the
learner selects an endpoint with at least 0.25 m task-normal displacement, a
route-consistent side commitment removes opposite-side endpoints on subsequent
active-yield retriggers.  Its progress horizon is configuration controlled; it
also releases after a path-tangent change greater than $45^{\circ}$, a newly
observed conflict on the committed side, loss of every planning-safe escape on
that side, or terminal reset.

If an unresolved interaction reaches its second recovery activation, the
recurrent-escape constraint retains only already-valid lateral subgoals and
REPLAN whenever at least one exists.  Reliable unilateral closing-flow evidence
applies the same escalation on the first activation.  If no such escape is
legal and BACKUP remains the selected safe fallback, unilateral flow uses a
1-s bounded pulse before recomputing the complete mask; ordinary BACKUP remains
bounded by 3 s.  These observable filters only intersect the geometric and
planning mask, never re-enable motion, and retain WAIT as a fail-closed
fallback.  Their thresholds were selected on validation interactions; we make
no isolated causal or formal-safety claim for any guard.

An independent supervisor may override any policy output.  Its longitudinal
stopping distance is
\begin{equation}
 d_{stop}=\frac{v^2}{2a_b}+v\ell+m,
\end{equation}
with $a_b=0.8$ m/s$^2$, latency $\ell=0.15$ s, and margin $m=0.45$ m in the
recovery controller.  It provides bounded stop, reverse, turn, and forward
escape submodes, all logged separately from the learned action.  Translation
uses a 0.48-m omnidirectional stop threshold.  In-place turns use a separate
0.40-m swept-radius threshold for the 0.36-m circular evaluation footprint, so
a safe side wall cannot prohibit every bounded rotation.  This is an
empirical safety filter, not a formal guarantee.

\subsection{Privileged Planning Expert}
At training time the expert constructs local occupancy from the recorded LiDAR
and observes privileged robot and pedestrian positions.  Pedestrian velocities
are estimated by finite differences rather than read as simulator truth.  It
uses constant-velocity prediction with conservative yielding and uncertainty
inflation, then simulates every
legal action for 3 s at 0.1-s resolution.  Subgoal rollouts use grid A* and pure
pursuit with differential-drive kinematics; the four mode actions have matched
short-horizon models.  The cost is
\begin{align}
J(a)={}&w_cJ_c+w_pJ_p+w_sJ_s+w_rJ_r+w_lJ_l \\
      &+w_{sm}J_{sm}+w_tJ_t+w_{sw}J_{sw}+w_wJ_w,
\end{align}
where the terms denote collision, insufficient progress, social-distance
integral, path-rejoin distance, length, angular smoothness, time, side switching,
and repeated waiting.  The weights are respectively
$(10^6,8,4,3,0.4,0.2,0.3,0.5,1)$; collision and masked actions are effectively
hard constraints.  Besides the minimum-cost action, the expert stores all 25
costs and the gap to the second-best action for analysis.
Figure~\ref{fig:action-expert} is a schematic of this candidate ranking, not a
recorded episode rollout.

\subsection{Behavior Cloning and DAgger}
The policy uses two 1-D convolutions over the $5\times180$ LiDAR stack, a
128-unit state encoder, and a two-layer head, totaling 59,193 parameters.  For
the selected model, uniform behavior cloning minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}_{BC}=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\log\pi_{\theta}(a_i^*\mid o_i,M_i).
\end{equation}
DAgger then executes the learner on training scenarios, asks the privileged
expert to relabel visited recovery states, aggregates them, and retrains.  The
two-round workflow was completed, but the second-round candidate was not
selected on validation.  The deployed checkpoint instead retrains the accepted
DAgger aggregate after adding a train-only head-on coverage shard; no validation
or test episode enters that shard.  This selected aggregate contains 1,387 raw
recovery states from 12 episodes; mirror augmentation yields 2,774 training
samples, with 399 states in a scenario-disjoint validation set.  Training uses
AdamW at $3\times10^{-4}$, batch size 128, maximum 30 epochs, gradient norm 1,
and early-stopping patience 6.  The deployment checkpoint is selected by
validation loss and closed-loop validation behavior.  Its configuration uses
uniform BC and no PPO; throughout the paper, PGRR therefore denotes the
selected DAgger policy rather than an RL-refined variant.
