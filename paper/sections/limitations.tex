The study uses planar LiDAR, a known two-dimensional map, and simulation
localization that is more accurate than a deployed AMCL stack.  Its
deterministic pedestrians are useful for paired replay but do not capture human
intent, group norms, perception uncertainty, or the diversity of real crowds.
Minimum distance and discomfort time are therefore geometric proxies rather
than validated human-comfort judgments.

The detector, planning mask, learned policy, Nav2 controller, and emergency
supervisor act jointly in the deployed hierarchy.  Closed-loop outcomes do not
attribute an effect to any one component, and the stopping supervisor provides
no formal collision-avoidance guarantee.  The privileged expert depends on
simulator state and short-horizon motion prediction; its finite 25-action set
cannot express arbitrary reciprocal negotiation.  The expert is consequently
a training and diagnostic reference, not a certified optimal controller.

All final conditions use one pinned simulator/runtime profile.  We do not claim
cross-simulator transfer, hardware validation, PPO refinement, or a learned
failure detector.  Although five held-out repetitions improve on single-seed
cells, the benchmark still covers a limited set of maps, route templates, and
pedestrian behaviors.  Responsive human models, additional maps and planners,
formal safety analysis, and real-robot trials are the most important extensions.
