The study has several important limits.  First, the sensing interface is planar
LiDAR on a known two-dimensional map, and simulation localization is more
accurate than a deployed AMCL stack.  Second, the Gazebo fallback pedestrians
follow deterministic routes and use a simple yielding rule; they do not capture
human intent, group norms, perception uncertainty, or the diversity of real
crowds.  Minimum distance and discomfort time are therefore geometric proxy
metrics, not validated human-comfort judgments.

Third, the current operating point prioritizes contact suppression: 16 of 24
PGRR primary trials terminate at the evaluation horizon.  Because the detector,
mask, policy, and supervisor act jointly, this study cannot assign that behavior
to one component.  The privileged expert also depends on simulator truth and a
3-s constant-velocity rollout, which cannot represent prolonged reciprocal
negotiation; the finite action set and safety supervisor provide no formal
collision-avoidance guarantee.

The offline labeler and Heuristic baseline retain a legacy 270-degree angular
calibration for 180 bins sampled from the 360-degree sensor.  The deployed mask
uses scan metadata, and the CNN receives identical full-scan resampling during
training and test, but expert action costs may inherit this distortion.  We
preserve the frozen evaluation instead of altering the pipeline after test-set
inspection.  Finally, each family--density cell has one fixed seed.  The 24
primary pairs support cross-condition paired inference, not a low-variance
per-scenario estimate.  Real-robot validation, responsive human models, learned
failure prediction, and PPO refinement remain outside the evaluated scope.
